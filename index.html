It has a fucking ad

Look at this beautiful empty rectangle.

Advertisement

Your ad could go here. It's empty because nobody's paid for the fucker yet.

One box, fixed size, labeled so nobody thinks I personally vouch for foot cream.

Your site has six of these. One in the header, one in the sidebar, one after every paragraph, and a sticky one at the bottom that covers the close button on the other one. Then you act surprised when everybody installs uBlock. You did that.

If you're going to run ads anyway, don't be a slob about it:

  • Give the box a fixed size before the ad loads, so your page doesn't jump around and make people click shit by accident. 728×90 on desktop, 320×100 on phones.
  • One ad. Not six. And not in the middle of a goddamn sentence.
  • Put the ads.txt line your network gives you in a file at yoursite.com/ads.txt. Takes thirty seconds. You lose ads without it.
  • Load the ad script async. Your text shouldn't sit there waiting on some ad server three countries away.
  • Showing personalized ads to people in the EU or UK? You need their consent first. Yes, that means a cookie banner. I'm not your lawyer.

What does it pay? A couple bucks per thousand views if you're lucky, and half of you nerds probably block it anyway. Also, Google limits ads on pages that swear a lot. Take a wild guess how that'd go for this page.

It takes tips

This one works even when your traffic is shit. Put a button up, and people who like your stuff can throw you a few bucks. No ad network has to approve you, and nobody gets tracked.

Ad networks won't touch this site. You might.

☕ Buy me a coffee

Buy Me a Coffee keeps 5% and the card company takes its bite. You get the rest. You won't get rich off it, but it doesn't need a million visitors before it pays a dime.

It'll have affiliate links

You link to stuff you actually use, and when somebody buys it through your link, the company pays you a cut. Web hosts pay the best, some of them $50 or more per signup. That's why the internet has ten thousand shitty "best web hosting" listicles.

The part you can't skip: say it's a paid link, right next to the link. Not in your footer. The FTC says "affiliate link" on its own isn't clear enough, but "paid link" is. Two words. You can manage two fucking words.

No affiliate links here yet. When there's one, it'll say "paid link" right next to it like a decent motherfucker.

The box is for sale

Ad networks take their cut and pay you pennies. You can skip them. Find a company your readers would actually give a shit about, charge them a flat price every month, and stick their banner in the box. You make more per view and there's no tracking script. Finding someone who'll pay is the hard part.

Want the box up top? It's for sale.

Sell a fucking thing

Ebooks, templates, stickers, a course about how to sell courses. If you've made something people want, selling it beats everything else on this page put together. I haven't. You probably haven't either.

Start a newsletter, I guess

Sponsors pay something like ten times more per reader to get into an inbox than onto a banner. The catch is you need a list first, and building one takes fucking forever.

Shit you should never do

  • Click your own ads. The network will catch you, and then it'll ban you.
  • Ask people to click your ads. Also gets you banned.
  • Popups. Nobody in the history of the internet has been happy to see a fucking popup.
  • Run a crypto miner in your visitors' browsers. You'll make eleven cents and their laptop will sound like a jet engine.
  • Sell your visitors' data. Don't be that motherfucker.

Yes, this is still fucking satire

I'm not saying don't make money off your site. Make all the money you want. I'm saying you don't have to wreck the thing to do it. Nobody ever closed a tab over one labeled ad and a coffee button. It's the popup and the autoplay video and the four other banners that make people leave.

And unless you've got real traffic, none of this pays your rent. It might cover the domain. Check the scoreboard.
